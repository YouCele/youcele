# 📂 Projects

## ⚡ Energy Forecasting

One of the projects I am particularly proud of is an **energy forecasting project**.

### What I'm most proud of

The part I am most proud of is taking a real-world forecasting problem and turning it into a structured data science problem. It required me to think about the data, identify useful patterns, build models, and evaluate whether the predictions were actually meaningful.

For me, the most rewarding part was seeing how historical data could be transformed into forecasts that could potentially support better planning and decision-making.

### The hardest part

The hardest part was making sure that the model was learning **real patterns rather than simply fitting the available data**.

Forecasting is different from a normal prediction problem because time matters. Data has to be organized correctly, historical information must be respected, and the model needs to be evaluated in a way that reflects how it would actually be used in the future.

This made me pay much more attention to **data preparation, feature engineering, validation, and model evaluation**.

# 🧠 How I Approach Problems

One thing that describes me well is that I am **very careful and cautious when working on a problem**.

Before jumping into a solution, I like to understand what I am actually dealing with: where the data comes from, what each variable means, what assumptions are being made, and what limitations the analysis might have.

I believe that a good model is not enough by itself. You also need to understand the **data, the assumptions, the evaluation process, and the context of the problem**.

I'm also working on improving one of my weaknesses: **organizing and explaining my ideas clearly**. I sometimes have many ideas at once and can struggle to structure them into a simple explanation. Because of this, I have been trying to become more deliberate about documenting my work and explaining the reasoning behind my decisions.
